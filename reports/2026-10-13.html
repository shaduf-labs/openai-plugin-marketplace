Using the task guide: decisions, safeguards and unresolved conflicts

Scope: the task pages and dated evidence linked below. Apply each recommendation only to its stated surface, channel and audience. A documented option is not proof of eligibility, installed capability or successful operation.

Choose the task you need to complete, then open its detailed page. Read the evidence date, the applicable scope, the next action and the condition that requires you to stop or ask for clarification.

Open the dated task guide → The guide supports decisions; it does not establish tested utility, a successful repair or demonstrated demand.

How to use the guide

First identify the host or product surface, submission channel and intended audience. Follow the task's essential action and stated safeguards. If access, authorization, required configuration or an action's outcome is uncertain, use the task's stopping condition rather than assume a successful result.

Decisions and safeguards

  1. Choose the relevant dated decision. Business explains the 12 October publication/placement distinction; Submit covers 11 October pending review and supported replacement; Submit locates the 10 October provided-client configuration and supporting 9 October auth detail. Use the 7 October CLI diagnostic for a missing capability and 8 October teammate continuity for an Enterprise handoff. The dated reports give the source comparisons and their limits.
  2. Keep design proposals separate from observed behavior. Architecture describes a conditional local dependency design, subject to the 6 October public-ZIP gate. Security describes a hypothetical fixed-action human approval/recovery design; Business presents six hypothetical commerce cases. Maintenance and Test provide reusable fixture kits, finite test scope and blank actual-host records.
  3. Use the exact boundary for each action. The 10 October OAuth guidance distinguishes the exact namespace, conditional secret and saved registration state. The 11 October review guidance distinguishes keeping the current published version from directly publishing an approved replacement. The 12 October visibility guidance distinguishes the exact publication name/portal link from discretionary directory placement. Human consent, current rights, access and authorization still matter. Stop redispatch when an external action's outcome is unknown; a route list cannot replace these conditions.
  4. Keep all four source disagreements visible. Public-ZIP conversion, annotation justification, checkout scope and MCP endpoint changes remain unresolved. Use the task and dated comparison links below; none establishes an exception or permission to bypass a gate.
  5. Read historical claims by their dates. The 9 October client-location uncertainty and 2 October broad retention uncertainty belong to their dated evidence. The active task guidance includes the more specific 10/11 October corrections. This does not establish that external policies have stayed unchanged since those source dates.

Evidence limits
